preserveCardinality
Benchmark overview

Pushing assertions down makes the rewrite usable; the engine decides the rest

A query over virtual RDF views can be answered by rewriting it into a query over the source data, or by materialising the views first and querying those. This page compares the two on three workloads and seven SPARQL engines: one consistent run of every engine, rewriter version and setting, measured 28 October – 1 November 2026.

Findings

What the run shows

÷65 – ÷541 on OAEI

Pushing assertions down is the pass that makes the rewrite work

On OAEI it makes every engine 65 to 541 times faster than the pipeline before it; on LUBM 13 to 68 times. On QLever, and on Blazegraph, the pipeline before it answers nothing at all, and on Virtuoso 2 of 52 LUBM queries.

Each pass, per engine
÷1.3 – ×3.3 on OAEI

Translating vocabularies costs little over materialised views

On OAEI the full pipeline runs within about three times the materialised views on every engine, at par on Jena, and faster than them on Oxigraph. Each query touches a few of 148 views, and the pushdown turns them into indexed lookups.

Cost per engine and scale
×0.9 – ×207 on LUBM

Reasoning by rewriting depends on the engine

On LUBM the same rewrites run at par with the materialised views on Oxigraph, four to five times slower on Virtuoso, and 10 to 35 times slower on QLever as data grows to 500 universities. Jena goes from 6 times slower at one university to 207 times at a hundred, GraphDB from 9 to 141 times.

Cost per engine and scale
≈17 queries, median

Materialising usually pays off within tens of queries

Building the views once beats rewriting after a median of 17 queries across the 44 engine-and-scale cells. Oxigraph is the exception: on LUBM from 10 universities up, the rewrite is faster on average, so the build never pays off.

0 wrong answers from the rewriter

Every wrong answer is a Virtuoso bug

All 321 wrong answers come from two Virtuoso bugs, both reproduced in isolation. With preserveCardinality on, every other answer is exact, except on StarBench queries whose hand-written reference is itself broken; there all engines return the same row count. With it off, LUBM answers repeat rows the views derive more than once.

Correctness per engine
47 → 52 GraphDB, LUBM

preserveCardinality completes GraphDB, is mostly neutral on LUBM and OAEI, and costs on StarBench

With it, GraphDB answers every LUBM query, 1.6 times faster. On LUBM and OAEI it changes most other engines' times by ÷1.1 to ×1.2, costs QLever 1.4 times on LUBM, and speeds Virtuoso up only on the plans that hit its row-losing bug. On StarBench it makes every engine 1.1 to 1.3 times slower and changes no answer.

The option per engine
÷3.8 – ÷8.3 on StarBench

On StarBench the pushdown matters as much, and hand-written SPARQL stays ahead

Pushing assertions down makes Oxigraph, Jena and Comunica 4 to 8 times faster on SPARQL 1.2 queries over reification and singleton properties. Comunica then answers 21 of 24 reification queries instead of 12, and 19 singleton ones instead of 4. The full pipeline still runs 4.5 to 13 times behind a hand-written query on Oxigraph and Jena (reification), and 26 to 35 times on Comunica.

Each pass, per engine
both ways flattening the subqueries

Whether flattening the subqueries pays depends on the engine

Every pipeline also ran with removeProjections appended; it changes no answer. Before the pushdown it makes Jena 1.2 to 1.3 times faster and GraphDB's remaining answers 2 to 4 times, but loses many answers on GraphDB, Oxigraph and Virtuoso. After it, keeping the subqueries is 1.3 to 2.9 times faster on QLever's, GraphDB's and Blazegraph's LUBM, and GraphDB answers 47 LUBM queries instead of 21 to 24. Flattening wins more queries than it loses on Jena, Comunica, and Blazegraph's and QLever's OAEI, single queries by up to 13 times. The library's default pipeline keeps the subqueries.

Setup

What is compared, on what, and how

Three rewrite pipelines, each the one before plus one pass, and each also flattened

1rewritingUnfold every view the query touches into a UNION of its CONSTRUCT bodies, each in a sub-SELECT, with FILTER(sameTerm(…)) for every constant.
→
2+pushDownAssertionsPush each sameTerm filter into the triple patterns, turning a scan into an indexed lookup.
→
3+pullUpExtendsFloat the BINDs the pushdown leaves at every leaf back up, or drop them. This is the order of the library's default pipeline.

Each pipeline also ran with removeProjections appended, right after it: it flattens the sub-SELECTs, and on pipeline 3 is followed by a second pullUpExtends. Question 7 compares the two.

materialised The reference. LUBM and OAEI: the original query over the views, materialised ahead of time (the build time is measured separately). StarBench: a hand-written SPARQL 1.1 query over the source.
preserveCardinality A rewriter option that wraps each view pattern in SELECT DISTINCT, so duplicates the views would not produce do not appear. Every rewrite runs with it off and on, back to back.

Three workloads

Protocol

  • Engines:
  • StarBench needs SPARQL 1.2, so it runs on Oxigraph, Jena and Comunica only. LUBM u250/u500 run on QLever only.
  • Budget: 60 s per query for LUBM and OAEI, 300 s for StarBench. A query that timed out at one LUBM scale is not run at the larger ones (the data nests).
  • Repeats: a query that finishes under 10 s runs 3 times and reports the median; a slower one runs once. Timeouts are never repeated.
  • Views: the standard RDF view (generalizedRdfView: false), the library's default.
  • Grading: every answer is compared with the reference as a bag (exact) and as a set (same rows, possibly repeated). StarBench ships some broken reference queries; answers that differ from those are marked as such, not as wrong.
  • Materialisation build times are from the earlier measurement (materialization.json); the rewriter does not affect them.

Question 5

Are the answers right?

The rewriter gave no wrong answer on any engine but Virtuoso, and Virtuoso's come from two bugs, each reproduced on a minimal query.

A filter dropped (rewriting, kept or flattened). Virtuoso drops FILTER(sameTerm(?o, C)) over a UNION whose branches bind ?o to different constants, and returns the whole union: many OAEI queries come back with 8,699 or 80,393 rows whatever they ask. ?o = C is answered correctly, and the pushdown removes these filters.

Rows lost (the four pushdown pipelines, preserveCardinality on). Joining three or more SELECT DISTINCT subqueries loses rows at VectorSize = 1. The default vector size answers those correctly but has a bug of its own on the rewrites without the option, so no setting is right for every rewrite. The benchmark keeps 1.

Repeats are expected. With preserveCardinality off, a LUBM fact the ontology derives in several ways appears several times. The rows are right; their multiplicity is not.

Some of StarBench's references are broken. Nine of its twelve hand-written singleton queries ask a different question than the SPARQL 1.2 query: other IRIs or concepts, or a missing pattern. The F-Q1 and F-Q2 references of both encodings return one variable too many (SELECT *). Answers that differ from these references are counted as broken ref., not wrong. All 579 such answers return the same number of rows on every engine and every pipeline. The nine singleton queries are also left out of every time comparison with the reference.

All six pipelines counted together. Exact: the same rows the same number of times. Repeats: the same rows, some repeated (expected with preserveCardinality off, as the views overlap). Ungraded: the reference itself timed out; the row count is then checked against the other engines' references. Broken ref.: differs from a StarBench reference known to be broken.

Question 6

What does preserveCardinality change?

The option wraps each view's pattern in SELECT DISTINCT, so an answer holds a row as often as the virtual graph would. Every rewrite ran both ways, back to back.

On GraphDB it completes LUBM: 52 queries instead of 47, 1.6 times faster. On a flattened rewrite GraphDB distributes a join over the top-level UNIONs. Each distributed join then shares no variable, only two BINDs, and becomes a cross product. Any subquery blocks that: the sub-SELECTs the default pipeline keeps, and the option's DISTINCTs. Flattened and without the option, GraphDB answers only 21 to 24 LUBM queries.

On LUBM and OAEI it is otherwise close to neutral. It helps where the views overlap and duplicates multiply, and costs where a large pattern meets a selective one. On QLever's LUBM the costs win, 1.4 times. Virtuoso runs LUBM 2.4 times faster with it, but in the form that loses rows.

On StarBench it only costs. With it on, the full pipeline is 1.1 to 1.3 times slower on all three engines, and it changes no answer: StarBench's views never produce a row twice, so there are no repeats to remove. Most of the difference shows at the last pass: +pullUpExtends gains ÷1.1 to ÷1.5 with the option off, but at most ÷1.2 with it on.

The full pipeline with the option on against off, paired query by query: the geometric mean over the queries both settings answered, all scales pooled. Below it, queries answered off → on.

Question 7

What is flattening the subqueries worth?

The unfolding wraps each view branch in a sub-SELECT. removeProjections flattens them into one tree; the library's default pipeline leaves it out. Every pipeline also ran with it appended, right after it, with the option off and on, and the reference graded both. No answer changes.

Before the pushdown it helps some engines a lot and breaks others. Jena runs LUBM and OAEI 1.2 to 1.3 times faster flattened, and GraphDB the queries it still answers 2 to 4 times. But GraphDB then answers 8 LUBM queries instead of 21 and 77 OAEI queries instead of 128, Oxigraph's LUBM is 1.8 times slower, and Virtuoso's state-slot limit rejects many flattened OAEI queries. Blazegraph answers 34 OAEI queries only flattened.

After it, the engines lean one way or the other.

  • Keeping is faster on LUBM for QLever, GraphDB and Blazegraph: 1.3 to 2.9 times with the option off. Flattened, GraphDB answers 21 to 24 LUBM queries instead of 47.
  • Flattening wins more queries than it loses on Jena, Comunica, and Blazegraph's and QLever's OAEI. At pipeline 3 with the option off: Jena 15 against 2 on StarBench reification, Comunica 44 against 13 on OAEI, Blazegraph 56 against 28. Most gains are small; single queries gain up to 13 times (Blazegraph's author_of_inv_talk_abstract).
  • The largest is F-Q1, which filters on regex(str(?source), …): Jena 5.2 s → 0.5 s on the singleton encoding. The gain comes from the second pullUpExtends the flattening allows, which moves the BIND of ?source above the filter, so the filter tests the triple pattern's own variable. Flattening alone, at pipeline 2, changes nothing there.

With the option on, the differences after the pushdown shrink to about 1.1 times, except QLever's and Blazegraph's LUBM. Since the gains from keeping the subqueries are larger and wider, the library's default pipeline (createDefaultTransformationPipeline) leaves removeProjections out. It is still exported, for engines and queries like these.

Engines

Configuration, limits and bugs

Every engine runs with server-wide settings only: no per-query hints or pragmas.

Oxigraph 0.5.11

RocksDB store built with oxigraph load, served read-only.
  • limitIts own materialised reference times out on two LUBM queries at u50 and seven at u100 (the views graph is 1.4 times the source), where the rewrite answers. Those answers are ungraded; with preserveCardinality on their row counts match QLever's and Jena's references.

Jena Fuseki 6.3.0-SNAPSHOT

TDB2 store built with tdb2.tdbloader --loader=parallel. A nightly: no release has the ARQ triple-term fix StarBench needs.
  • limitBefore the pushdown, kept or flattened, it answers no LUBM query from u50 up.

QLever build 8303d22 · 30 Sep 2026

Index built with qlever-index; query memory capped at 12 GB.
  • limitNo sameTerm, so rewriting fails on every query, kept or flattened; the pushdown removes the function.
  • limitLUBM Q09 without preserveCardinality: flattened, it runs out of the 12 GB query memory from u10 up; kept, it answers u10 in 50 s and times out beyond.
  • No RDF 1.2, so no StarBench. The only engine run at u250 and u500.

Virtuoso 7.2.17 open source

VectorSize = 1, MaxMemPoolSize = 16G, 50 M result rows allowed. Data in a named graph, which every query sets as its default graph.
  • bugFILTER(sameTerm(?o, C)) over a UNION that binds ?o to different constants is dropped: wrong answers before the pushdown.
  • bugAt VectorSize = 1, a join of three or more SELECT DISTINCT subqueries loses rows: 19 wrong answers per pushdown pipeline with preserveCardinality on, 76 in all.
  • bugAt the default vector size, a join over UNIONs that bind the join variable takes another branch's values; this is why it runs at 1, costing 5 to 20 times the speed.
  • limitCompiled-in limits: generated SQL over 10,000 lines (SP031), too many state slots (SQ156). These reject the larger rewrites before the pushdown.

GraphDB 11.5.1 Free

RDF4J 5.3.1 underneath; empty ruleset (no inference); 36 GB heap.
  • limitOn a flattened rewrite it distributes a join over top-level UNIONs (24 × 7 = 168 groups for LUBM Q05). Where the join key is only set by BIND, each group becomes a cross product. Any subquery stops it: the sub-SELECTs the default pipeline keeps, or preserveCardinality's DISTINCTs.
  • So flattening costs it answers at every pipeline: LUBM 47 → 21 to 24 after the pushdown, OAEI 128 → 77 before it.

Blazegraph 2.1.6 RC

Triples mode, default settings, on Java 21.
  • limitThe optimiser puts patterns joined only through BIND-computed variables first, which makes cross products: most LUBM queries time out even after the pushdown.
  • limitIts stack overflows on the deeply nested plans before the pushdown.

Comunica 5.4.0

A long-lived worker per dataset: HDT (5.0.1) for LUBM and OAEI, an in-memory N3 store for StarBench.
  • limitMaterialising is very slow: 4 hours at u50 and 13 at u100, against minutes elsewhere. That is why its pay-off counts run into the hundreds.
  • limitOn StarBench it answers 4 to 12 of 24 queries before the pushdown and 19 to 22 after it. Many answers sit near the 300 s budget, so a run can turn one into a timeout or back: in October, re-running the singleton s block turned 5 timeouts into answers taking 108 to 288 s.
Method

How the numbers are made

How a cell is computed

Paired, per query. Every comparison is between two variants on the same query at the same scale. The cell shows the geometric mean of the time ratio over the queries both answered; the tooltip adds the median, the 10th–90th percentiles, and a win count.

Timeouts are censored, not dropped. A query one side answered and the other timed out on counts as a win for the side that answered, but adds no ratio. A query either side failed on with an engine error is left out and counted.

Ties. Within 10% counts as a tie: about the drift seen between runs of the same query.

Pooling. Where a cell pools scales, each query at each scale counts once. LUBM queries skipped at a larger scale, because they timed out at a smaller one, count as timeouts.

Pay-off. Build time divided by the mean saving per query of the materialised query over the full pipeline, over the queries neither errored on, with timeouts at the budget.

What is new in this version of the page

The ladder now follows the library's default pipeline, and every number comes from one new run: commit 84744a8 (the rewriter's passes unchanged since 20181c2), the same harness and settings for every engine, measured 28 October – 1 November 2026.

  • The pushdown pipelines no longer flatten. Until October, pipelines 3 and 4 ran removeProjections; the default pipeline dropped it after a run found keeping the subqueries faster on most engines. The old pipelines are still measured, as the flattened twins of pipelines 2 and 3, and +removeProjections before the pushdown as the twin of pipeline 1.
  • Against the October run, 12,893 of the 12,996 rows the two share agree in status, row count and grade. The rest are queries near the budget, and answers left ungraded because a reference timed out.
  • Standard RDF view (generalizedRdfView: false), preserveCardinality on and off back to back per query, and the median of three for queries under 10 s, as in October. Compare ratios within this page, not times across pages.
Not measured
  • StarBench on QLever, Virtuoso, GraphDB and Blazegraph: none supports SPARQL 1.2.
  • StarBench's larger scales (m, l), and LUBM above u100 on any engine but QLever.
  • The two steps of the library's default pipeline the ladder leaves out: expanding property paths first and nullifyJoinOverIncompatibleBounds last. Neither changes any of the benchmark's rewrites.
  • Cold-cache timings: every engine is warm after loading, and fast queries report the median of three runs.
Reproducing it

Per engine, from the repository root, with the engines set up as in test/bench/README.md:

npx tsx test/bench/run.ts --engines oxigraph --schemes lubm,oaei \
  --scales u1,u10,u50,u100,eswc,d100 --timeout 60000 \
  --rdf-views standard --paired-cardinality --reps 3 --reps-below 10000 \
  --out test/bench/results/bench-ladder-oxigraph-workloads.json

npx tsx test/bench/run.ts --engines oxigraph --schemes reification,singleton \
  --scales xs,s --timeout 300000 \
  --rdf-views standard --paired-cardinality --reps 3 --reps-below 10000 \
  --out test/bench/results/bench-ladder-oxigraph-starbench.json
